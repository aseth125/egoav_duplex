Interaction counting — end-to-end design

Repeated drawer openings, phone pickups, and distinct physical objects for an egocentric assistant.

Status: design plus a narration-level feasibility census on 20 episodes (2026-10-06). No counting item is released. The earlier 90–95% figure was a planning estimate, not measured precision. It must never appear in a result table as an observed score.

The release target is at least 95% precision after independent double audit, with every disagreement resolved before release. Phone yield and phone precision are unknown pending a wider visual pilot.

Version-1 scope: drawer-opening transitions and phone pickups. Cabinet, structural-door, appliance-door, and access-episode numbers below are feasibility-census results only. They do not become benchmark families until they have their own state machines, fixtures, and audit.

1. What this family tests

A glasses wearer should be able to ask a model to count what has happened, without mentally keeping a tally while doing something else.

“Since I started making lunch, how many times have I opened a drawer?” → “Four times.”

“Since I started cooking, how many different drawers did I open?” → “Three.”

“Since I sat down here, how many times have I picked up the phone?” → “Twice.”

There are two separate arms:

armunitexample
Repeated event countEvery qualifying state transition. Reopening the same drawer after a verified close counts again.One drawer opened three times → answer 3.
Unique physical object countDistinct physical identities that had at least one qualifying transition.Three openings across two drawers → answer 2.

These arms have different family labels and score reports. A question must say times or different; “How many drawers did I open?” is rejected because either answer could be defensible.

2. What the 20-episode pilot actually measured

The census read all 1,662 original Nymeria narration rows across 20 episodes / 4,380 seconds, then clustered duplicate and overlapping paraphrases. These are proposals from text, not strict visual ground truth.

The two flagged drawer proposals are a clip-boundary event and a pullout called both a drawer and an organizer. They are not in the count.

The extracted tracks do not solve identity: Storage has 325 tracks but merges shelves, cabinets, drawers, dressers, and other fixtures; Door has 171 tracks but its aliases include door casings and window frames. There is no phone track class, and Screen explicitly excludes mobile-phone screens. Track counts therefore cannot be reported as unique drawer, cabinet, door, or phone counts.

Three frame spot checks support the gate design: an Amy Snow oven drawer visibly transitions from closed to open; a Grace utensil-drawer window starts after the drawer is already open and cannot prove the transition; an Amanda “organizer under the wall cabinet” example shows a box interaction while the cabinet remains closed. These cases are examples, not a measured pass rate.

Pilot artifacts: machine-readable census, row-level clustering audit, and feasibility notes.

3. The counting ontology

Every item fixes five definitions before mining:

  1. Actor: the wearer only, unless an arm explicitly studies other people. A colleague opening a cabinet does not enter “How many did I open?”
  2. Object class: version 1 supports a drawer leaf or phone. Generic storage and door never ship. The other classes in the census remain proposals until separately specified.
  3. Interval: the half-open interval [count_start_s, question_start_s). question_start_s is the first sample of wearer speech; question_end_s is stored separately. The stable post-state of every counted event must finish before question_start_s.
  4. Unit: repeated transitions or unique physical identities. The unit is stored and stated in the question.
  5. Referent: singular wording such as “the phone” or “my phone” binds to one verified physical_object_id. If ownership or identity is not grounded, the wording changes or the item declines.

Leaf transitions, access episodes, and unique objects are never aliases. For example, two cabinet leaves opened in one visit can be two leaf transitions and one access episode, but neither count ships under version 1.

4. Exact drawer-opening state machine

Each physical drawer leaf has its own state path:

UNKNOWN → CLOSED → OPENING → OPEN → CLOSING → CLOSED

statevisual condition
UNKNOWNIdentity or aperture cannot be judged because of occlusion, crop, blur, or a clip boundary.
CLOSEDThe front is seated against the fixture and remains so for at least 8 frames on the 15 fps evidence stream.
OPENINGThe same leaf moves outward and its gap/interior increases for at least two ordered frames.
OPENA visible cavity or usable access aperture follows an intentional outward pull and remains stable for at least 8 frames, or the wearer reaches into the newly exposed interior while it remains open. Mechanical play or a hairline gap is insufficient.
CLOSINGThe same leaf moves inward and its aperture decreases.

A drawer-open event is emitted only for CLOSED → OPENING → OPEN. Its event time is the first frame after the final closed frame where outward motion begins, conditional on the later stable OPEN state.

  • A handle touch, failed tug, “about to open,” or pull that returns without reaching OPEN counts zero.
  • An accidental partial displacement without a visible cavity or usable opening is rejected or quarantined; it is never rounded up.
  • Holding an already-open drawer counts zero.
  • Pausing during one pull does not create another event.
  • A second event requires a verified stable CLOSED reset before the next opening.
  • Two leaves moving during one visit are two leaf transitions and one access episode.
  • A drawer already open at interval start may later contribute a reopening, but its initial unseen opening does not count.
  • Appliance-internal drawers are a separate subtype and are never silently mixed with furniture drawers.

The 8-frame holds are the initial operational rule at 15 fps. Before release they are stress-tested against faster and slower transitions. If a valid event cannot satisfy them because of occlusion or boundary censoring, the item declines; the threshold is not relaxed per item.

4.1 When to ask

For every positive drawer item, the question is sited immediately after the latest qualifying opening has finished. The drawer must already have reached its verified stable OPEN state before the first sample of question speech. An onset gap of 0.5–3.0 seconds after post-state confirmation is a hard version-1 gate.

  • post_state_end_s < question_start_s is a hard gate.
  • 0.5 ≤ question_start_s - post_state_end_s ≤ 3.0 is a hard gate.
  • No intervening task action of any class may occur in the gap; intervening_action_status must be NONE. Incidental head or hand settling is not a task action.
  • The question may begin while the drawer remains open; opening it is already a completed action.
  • If the wearer begins asking while the drawer is still moving, move the question later or reject the item. Do not use later frames to retroactively prove it.
  • A later question is rejected from version 1 or labeled as a separate delayed-memory counting family; a written exception cannot make it an immediate item.

This makes the natural trigger “I just did that—how many times have I done it?” while keeping every counted action in the past. For a repeated count, the answer still covers the declared interval, including earlier verified openings.

5. Exact phone-pickup state machine

Phone pickup is a support transfer, not a mention, gaze, tap, or hand proximity. The state path is:

ABSENT/UNKNOWN → ON_EXTERNAL_SUPPORT_RELEASED → GRASPING → HAND_SUPPORTED → PLACING_DOWN → ON_EXTERNAL_SUPPORT_RELEASED

statevisual condition
ABSENTNo phone is visible in the review region. Absence is not a support state and cannot begin a pickup proof.
UNKNOWNPhone identity, support contact, or the receiving hand is not visible enough to decide.
ON_EXTERNAL_SUPPORT_RELEASEDThe same phone rests on a table, counter, charger, shelf, seat, or other non-hand support for at least 8 frames, with no wearer hand contact or hand control.
GRASPINGThe wearer's hand establishes contact while external support may still remain.
HAND_SUPPORTEDExternal support has ended and the phone remains grasped or co-moves with the hand for at least 8 frames.
PLACING_DOWNExternal support has resumed but the wearer may still touch or control the phone; this is not yet a reset.

A phone-pickup event is emitted only for ON_EXTERNAL_SUPPORT_RELEASED → GRASPING → HAND_SUPPORTED. Its event time is the first frame after final external-support contact, conditional on the stable hand-supported state.

  • Touching, tapping, swiping, plugging in, or looking at a phone that stays supported counts zero.
  • Repositioning fingers while the phone remains hand-supported counts zero.
  • Putting the phone down and picking it up again counts a second event only after PLACING_DOWN → ON_EXTERNAL_SUPPORT_RELEASED: at least 8 frames of external support with the wearer no longer touching or controlling it.
  • Already holding the phone at interval start does not create a pickup.
  • A peer-to-wearer handoff is stored as handoff_acquire, not phone pickup, unless a separate handoff arm is released.
  • A pocket or bag extraction is rejected when the phone's pre-state and identity are hidden. It can become a future subtype after a dedicated pilot.

For “my phone” or “the phone,” every included event must bind to the same verified phone identity. The item records how that referent was grounded, such as the wearer placing the device earlier. Visual association does not prove legal ownership, so “the phone” is preferred when ownership is not established. If two phones could satisfy the wording, the item is rejected rather than summed.

The 20-episode narration census produced zero phone-pickup candidates. That is evidence about the narration, not evidence that no phone pickup occurred. Phone yield, detector recall, and precision remain pending a wider visual pilot.

6. Candidate proposals and duplicate clustering

Narration, object tracks, motion proposals, and a phone detector may propose an event. None may certify one. Proposal rows are deliberately over-complete and then merged before visual review.

  1. Extract explicit transition language and retain the full source row and time span.
  2. Generate visual motion proposals independently, including proposals with no narration row.
  3. Group proposals by episode, family, candidate identity, and overlapping transition interval.
  4. Merge exact-time annotator paraphrases and progressive continuations with no intervening reset.
  5. Keep separate events when text says another, identity changes, a verified close/reset intervenes, or the wearer revisits later.
  6. Send label conflicts, clip-boundary events, and actor conflicts to quarantine with their evidence.

The pilot examples show why this is required: three rows can describe one oven-drawer opening, while one row can describe two different drawer openings.

evidence_cutoff_s must equal question_start_s. The sweep manifest records expected and decoded frame ranges, gaps, controls, reviewer, and escalation regions. Excluded and ambiguous proposals stay in the ledger so a zero or low count can be audited.

physical_object_id is episode-local and visual. Track UID is stored as a proposal attribute, never copied into this field automatically. Split tracks of the same drawer are merged; two adjacent leaves are separate identities. An identity merge needs matching geometry, location, appearance, and continuity across the relevant views. Unresolved identity means a generic repeated-drawer arm may survive while the unique-object arm declines; a singular-phone item also declines because its referent requires identity.

8. Dense verification and the full-interval negative sweep

Verifying the proposed positives is only half the work. A count of 3 is wrong if a fourth event was never proposed. Every counting item therefore needs both gates:

gatequestion answeredrequirement
positive transition gateDid each ledger event really occur?Dense pre-state, transition, and post-state frames satisfy the state machine.
full-interval negative sweepWas any other qualifying event missed?The complete count interval is screened for the target family, including periods with no narration.

The default dense evidence stream is 15 fps. A human reviewer covers the complete stream continuously, using video playback plus the indexed dense frames; detector proposals may focus inspection but never replace the full sweep. The manifest lists every expected presentation-time range, every decoded frame, reviewer identity, review status, and any dropped/decode gap. Coarse 2 fps frames may prioritize candidate regions but cannot certify completeness. Fast phone transfers, quick open-close motion, blur, or disagreement are rechecked at source frame rate.

A detector's silence is accepted only after a present control in the same episode proves that its prompt can find the target at a comparable scale, view, lighting, and occlusion level. The same prompt and threshold are used in the sweep. For drawers, motion and aperture proposals supplement semantic detection. For phones, both a whole-phone detector and hand/contact reasoning are required.

If the control fails, tracking loses identity through a possible transition, or any section of the interval cannot be decoded, the item declines. Missing evidence is never interpreted as zero.

One verified episode/family ledger and coverage manifest may serve several nested question intervals. Each QA view cites the exact covered prefix; overlapping questions do not trigger duplicate decoding or review, and no item may cite frames outside the reviewed coverage.

9. Unique physical object arm

This arm asks how many different objects had a qualifying event, not how many objects exist in the room. Counting all drawers in a room would require proving the wearer saw every drawer, including closed and off-camera fixtures; this dataset cannot support that claim.

“How many different drawers did I open?” is supported.

“How many drawers were in the kitchen?” is unsupported unless a separate exhaustive scene-inventory protocol is built.

The answer is count(distinct physical_object_id) over verified events in the interval. The repeated-event answer is count(event_id). The two are computed from the same frozen ledger.

10. Questions and answers

10.1 Question forms

Questions are short, spoken, and explicit about unit and interval:

  • “Since I started making lunch, how many times did I open a drawer?”
  • “Since I started cooking, how many different kitchen drawers did I open?”
  • “Since I sat down at the desk, how many times did I pick up the phone?”

The interval anchor must be a completed, visually verified, uniquely identifiable event. If the wearer sat down twice, “after I sat down” is ambiguous unless the location or occurrence resolves it. “So far” is allowed only when the media begins at a natural session or activity boundary visible to the wearer; a benchmark crop boundary is not a conversational anchor.

Rejected: “How many did I open?” (missing class), “How many drawers?” (missing unit), “Did I open it once or twice?” (constrains and leaks the answer range), and any anchor that is ambiguous, unfinished, or later than the question. A constrained-choice count can be studied only as a separate reported arm.

10.2 Answer forms

The reference answer contains one numeric quantity for the requested unit. The structured answer_count is authoritative; the surface form stays short:

“Four times.”

“Three different drawers.”

“I can’t tell reliably because a pickup was hidden by the bag.”

Accepted numeric forms include digits, ordinary number words, “none,” and “didn’t … any” for zero. Declines use a fixed semantic class rather than a guessed number. A response with two incompatible numeric quantities is incorrect; secondary counts belong in a separate question.

No timestamps, hidden annotation terms, or future events appear in the answer.

11. Scoring

Primary scoring is exact count accuracy, reported separately for repeated-event, unique-object, and decline items. Parse digits and ordinary number words, but do not infer a count from vague language such as “a few.”

Aggregate scores must include answer-count histograms and per-subtype results. A model can look strong by predicting the mode in a skewed arm.

12. Zero counts, negatives, and declines

A zero-count item is valuable only when the full negative sweep passes. Narration silence alone does not license zero. Zero items should include nearby non-qualifying actions such as touching a handle, tapping a supported phone, opening a cabinet while the target is drawers, or a colleague performing the action.

A decline is the correct label when:

  • the interval begins or ends during a possible event;
  • pre-state, transfer, reset, or post-state is occluded;
  • object identity is lost or two leaves cannot be separated;
  • the source row and frames disagree on class or actor;
  • the detector control fails or the full sweep has a gap;
  • the repeated-event count is known but unique identity is unresolved.

Uncertain examples are kept in quarantine with a reason. They are not silently deleted and are not converted into zero.

13. Balance and answer priors

Balance is enforced per arm, not across the whole benchmark:

  • answer buckets 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4+;
  • drawer and phone for version 1; future object classes are balanced only inside their separately released families;
  • repeated-event versus unique-object questions;
  • short, medium, and long count intervals;
  • single-room versus multi-room intervals;
  • clean positives, hard negatives, and declines.

The large-corpus builder measures available yield before authoring QA, then pre-registers selection caps. The default release rule is that no single numeric answer may exceed 30% within a released arm, with declines reported outside the numeric histogram. If strict yield makes that impossible, the release publishes the deviation and the modal baseline; it does not invent or relabel examples. Phone cannot receive a target share until its visual pilot establishes yield.

14. Baselines

Every release reports:

  1. Always zero and modal count by arm — answer-prior baselines.
  2. Narration regex without dedupe — exposes duplicate-row inflation.
  3. Narration regex with temporal clustering — measures what text alone supplies.
  4. Raw track-row count — expected to fail the unique-object arm and documents why track UID is not identity.
  5. Visual proposals without the negative sweep — quantifies missed-event damage.
  6. Frozen event-ledger oracle — verifies scorer and question assembly.

Audio-only, video-only, and audio-video model results are reported separately to measure narration leakage.

15. Chaining, interruption, and leakage rules

  1. No future evidence. The interval is exactly [count_start_s, question_start_s). Every counted event's stable post-state must finish before question onset; neither answer nor proof may read question-time or later frames.
  2. One frozen ledger across variants. The no-interruption and interruption/backchannel versions use identical event IDs, interval, count, and answer.
  3. No assistant tally leakage. During the count interval, assistant speech may not enumerate, label, or acknowledge target events. A model-initiated reminder such as “you opened the drawer” makes a later drawer count easier and is either excluded or placed in a separately reported exposed stratum.
  4. Shared HOI evidence. If an nth-event reminder and a later count item coexist, both cite the same frozen event IDs and ledger hash. The item is reported in the reminder-exposed stratum even when the reminder was interrupted.
  5. No chain answer leakage. An earlier question, refinement, cross-question, or answer may not state the later count or list all contributing events.
  6. No critical visual masking. User interruption, subtitles, or overlays cannot cover the pre-state, transition, or post-state evidence. Audio overlap is allowed only after the zero-collision media gate.
  7. Interrupted answers remain scorable. The reference stores the count separately from its surface sentence. If the user interrupts after the number has been spoken, the count is delivered; otherwise the resume turn must still provide it.
  8. Natural anchors only. A chained question may refer to a completed earlier activity, never an event that occurs later.

16. Confidence and independent audit

Confidence has three separate fields. They use an auditable enum rather than an invented probability:

fieldmeaning
transition_confidencepre-state, motion/transfer, and post-state satisfy the state machine
identity_confidencethe physical object is stable across all contributing events
completeness_confidencethe full interval has been swept with a working control
valueoperational meaningeffect
PASSBoth auditors find the required evidence and no unresolved contradiction.Eligible for a strict numeric item.
NOT_REQUIREDThe field is outside the arm: cross-event identity for a generic repeated-drawer count only.Eligible when the arm definition permits it.
AMBIGUOUSEvidence could support more than one label because of occlusion, boundary, identity loss, failed control, or reviewer disagreement.Numeric item is barred; retain as a documented decline candidate or quarantine.
FAILThe evidence positively contradicts the proposed transition, identity, or completeness claim.Exclude the proposal and keep the failed record in the ledger.

The item's confidence is the minimum of the fields needed by its arm. A generic repeated-drawer count does not require cross-event identity when each transition and class is clear. A unique-object arm always does. A singular-phone question also requires the same phone identity and its referent/ownership basis across every included event.

A strict numeric item requires transition_confidence=PASS for every included event and completeness_confidence=PASS. It also requires identity_confidence=PASS for unique-object and singular-phone arms; only a generic repeated-drawer item may use NOT_REQUIRED. A decline item names the exact AMBIGUOUS field and reason. No FAIL proposal contributes to a gold count.

Release quality target: “precision” means item-level exact-gold correctness: correct numeric or decline label, complete included-event set, and no extra event. Every release item is independently double-audited. Auditor A and Auditor B inspect the source evidence and state path without seeing each other's label; a third reviewer adjudicates disagreements.

After corrections, a fresh blinded audit samples max(100 items, 10% of the release), stratified with at least 20 items per released family/arm when yield permits. The lower bound of a two-sided 95% Wilson interval on item correctness must be at least 0.95; otherwise the build remains a pilot. If the release is too small to support that bound, it reports the observed result without claiming 95%. Event-transition precision and missed-event recall from the independent re-sweep are also reported with confidence intervals.

The previous 90–95% statement remains an estimate. The first measured precision number comes from this audit and nowhere else.

19. Pipeline and scale to 400–500 episodes

#stageoutput
1immutable source inventoryepisode windows, media hashes, narration and track provenance
2text and visual proposal miningover-complete proposal rows
3dedupe and provisional identity clusteringcandidate clusters, never final counts
4dense state-machine verificationverified positives and quarantines
5full-interval negative sweepcompleteness verdict and detector controls

8chain and media collision gatepaired clean/interruption variants

10release freezechecksums, manifests, balance and baseline reports

Episodes are independent shards. GPU workers decode and propose; verification outputs are immutable JSONL plus frame manifests; a central reducer performs only schema checks, identity joins, balance, and release assembly. A failed shard is retried without changing completed shards. Every stage records code revision, model revision, prompt, thresholds, source hash, and output hash.

The run report records, per episode, family, and pipeline stage: source seconds, expected/decoded frames, GPU seconds, detector calls, reviewer minutes, proposal clusters, deduplicated candidates, accepted events, quarantines and exclusions by reason, complete count intervals, released items, and strict items per episode. It also reports total GPU-hours and reviewer-hours, yield per source hour, and cost per accepted strict item. Values remain blank until measured; planning estimates are labeled separately.

The episode/family ledger is computed once. Coverage manifests support verified prefixes and sub-intervals, so many QA views can reuse the same dense sweep without multiplying GPU or reviewer cost.

The pilot average is 219 seconds per episode, so a pilot-shaped 400–500 episode corpus would contain roughly 1.31–1.64 million frames at 15 fps per full visual stream. This is a workload calculation, not a yield or precision forecast. The 20 episodes were not established as a random sample of the future corpus.

Scale first on a visual pilot large enough to measure phone proposals, false negatives, state-machine disagreements, and dense-review time. Only then set throughput, GPU, and staffing estimates.
