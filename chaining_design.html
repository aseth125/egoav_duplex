chaining — turning a list of questions into a conversation

Status: design only (2026-10-05). Nothing built. Capacity numbers are measured off briefs_v4 tonight; the design is not.

Run the whole thing (once it exists): bash duplex/avd/run_chain_gates.sh → CHAIN_CHAIN_OK.

This document covers episode construction, not any one skill. The five skill documents say what a good item is. This one says how 3–10 of them become something that sounds like a person talking to an assistant rather than a quiz being administered.

Skill documents: EPISODIC_MEMORY_DESIGN · OBJECT_EPISODIC_MEMORY_DESIGN · INTERRUPTION_MODEL_HOI_DESIGN · ACTION_NARRATION_DESIGN · DURATION_ACTIVITY_DESIGN.

1. Two modes

modeshapewhat it measures
homogeneous3–10 turns, one skilldoes the skill hold up across an episode, or drift
heterogeneous3–10 turns, mixed skillscan the model switch tasks mid-conversation

Heterogeneous is the default because it is what makes an episode duplex. Homogeneous is a consistency test, not a realism test, and is sized accordingly — around a fifth of episodes.

2. Capacity — turn count follows clip length

Clips are not one length, and most of the corpus is short.

Capacity assumes 25 s of spacing and no turn before 45 s, because the memory and HOI families need a lead.

57% of the corpus is 180 s or shorter. A flat 3–10 target would either starve the long clips or be impossible on the short ones, so the turn count is set from the clip length: long clips carry the 8–10 turn episodes, short ones carry 3–4.

Heterogeneous material is plentiful — 294 of 477 clips offer all five families, and 138 of those are the 600 s clips, which are also the ones with room.

A pool that was generated by a script and is validated by reading the same script's output is not validated at all.

4. Ordering is a constraint, not a preference

Packing is constraint satisfaction over a timeline, not sampling:

  • an HOI standing instruction must come before its trigger, with a lead
  • a duration question must come after the activity ends
  • a memory question must come after the event it asks about
  • no turn may read material later than its own anchor
  • two turns may not occupy overlapping airtime

Build the packer as a timeline with reservations from the start. Retrofitting ordering onto a sampler produces episodes that are individually valid and collectively impossible.

5. Cross-turn leakage — the one that ruins it silently

In a multi-turn episode the model sees the earlier turns.

Turn 3 — "Where did I put the cup?" → "On the table." Turn 7 — "What was I doing four minutes ago?" → "Putting the cup on the table."

Turn 7 is now free. It is answerable from the transcript with the video switched off, and nothing about the item looks wrong.

Today this barely bites because episodes are sparse. At 8–10 turns it bites constantly, and it bites silently — the item passes every skill gate, the model scores well, and the score means nothing.

The gate: a text-only model is shown turns 1…N−1 and the question of turn N, with no video and no narration. Anything it answers correctly is leaked, and that turn is dropped or re-sited.

This is the main new engineering in episode construction, and it belongs to no single skill, which is why it lives here.

5.1 Leakage runs the other way too

Turn 3 can also leak backwards into turn 7's premise: once the model has been told the cup is on the table, a later HOI item "tell me when I pick up the cup" has had its referent located for free. The gate runs on every turn against every earlier turn, not on a sample.

6. Making it a conversation, not a list

A correct episode can still read like a form. These are the devices that make it a chain. Each one is optional per episode and counted after the fact, never assigned by position.

devicewhat happenswhy it is worth having
model interruptionthe model cuts in unprompted at a triggerthe HOI family; the only model-initiated turn
wearer interruptionthe wearer cuts the model off mid-answerthe model must stop, not finish its sentence
backchannel"mm-hm", "yeah", "right"not a turn; must not be answered
counter-questionthe model asks before answeringtests knowing when it cannot answer yet
repair"no, the other one"the model must revise, not restart
ellipsis / follow-up"and the other one?"the only place a bare pointer is legal
abandonmentthe wearer starts and drops a questionthe model must not answer the fragment
topic returnback to an earlier thread after a detourtests holding a thread across turns

6.1 Backchannel is not a turn

"mm-hm" while the model is talking is not a request. A model that treats it as one produces a non-sequitur, and a scorer that treats it as a turn marks the correct silence as a miss.

So backchannels are authored with scored: false and an explicit expected: no_response. They are the cheapest realism device available and they also catch a real failure.

6.2 Counter-question needs an authored reply, and the reply must not leak

If the model is expected to ask "the blue folder or the grey one?", the wearer's answer has to exist, and that answer must not contain the thing the model is about to be scored on. A counter-question whose reply gives away the target is a two-turn way of handing over the answer.

Counter-questions are only sited where the question is genuinely ambiguous — two candidate referents both live at that moment. Manufacturing ambiguity to create the device produces a turn that a good model is right to skip.

6.3 Repair must be a real correction

"No, I meant the one in the kitchen" requires that the model's first answer was wrong for a reason present in the clip — two candidates, and it picked the other one. A repair scripted onto a correct first answer teaches the model to abandon right answers when pushed.

6.4 Ellipsis is the one legal bare pointer

Every skill document forbids "where did I put it?" with no referent. In a chain, "and the other one?" immediately after a resolved turn is legal and is the point of having chains. The rule is adjacency: the referent must be resolvable from the immediately preceding exchange, not from four turns back.

6.5 cohesion must be derived, never assigned

The turn schema already has a cohesion field. It has previously been set by idx % 6 — a value written by a counter and then validated by a gate that reads the same value back. That is not a measurement.

cohesion is computed from the text relationship between a turn and its predecessor, and the gate recomputes it from the text rather than reading the field. Same rule for mode, renderer, and any other field a builder writes and a checker reads.

7. Visual verification

Every shipped turn carries a visual verdict, and no turn ships on narration or metadata alone. The skill documents each specify what their own check is; this document specifies the three that are chain-level.

  1. Candidate pools are verified, not trusted. proactive_candidates, memory_callback_candidates and attention_shifts were produced by scripts. Every one that becomes a turn gets its claim checked against frames — the object is where the pool says, at the time the pool says.
  2. Anchors are verified at the frame. A turn anchored at t is checked on the frames at t, not on the narration span containing t. Narration spans are 5 s and the anchor is a moment.
  3. The episode's whole timeline is checked once, not turn by turn in isolation: a frame pass that confirms turn 3's object is absent must not contradict turn 7's premise that it is present.

Where a visual check and the narration disagree, the turn is quarantined with the disagreement recorded. It is not resolved by preferring one source. Disagreement is information about the data, and overwriting it destroys the only signal that the pool is unreliable.

8. Mix targets are counted, never asserted

The packer aims at a mix of skills, a mix of devices, and for homogeneous episodes a mix of arms within the skill (present / absent / decline / negative). Eight turns of one skill with one arm teaches the answer shape; by turn six the model can answer without listening.

All of these are measured on the built corpus and reported. A target is an intention. The realised mix is the fact, and the two are routinely different.

11. Open

  1. The leak gate's text-only model and threshold are unchosen. Too strong a model drops valid turns; too weak a one passes leaks.
  2. How many turns survive the leak gate at 8–10 per episode is unknown and may be the binding constraint on episode length.
  3. Device rates — how often a backchannel or a repair should appear — are unset, and should come from looking at real assistant conversations rather than from a number chosen here.
  4. Whether the 25 s spacing in §2 is right. It was assumed, not measured.

Hard rules carried in
