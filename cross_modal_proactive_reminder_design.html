Cross-modal proactive reminders — end-to-end design

Prospective memory across vision and sound: visually arm a reminder now, then speak at the future cue.

Status · 2026-10-06: metadata census and first-pass candidate audit complete. No cue has been rendered and no item is Gold. Raw-source or final-mix audio review and independent second review remain required.

1. Task

The wearer grounds a future reminder while handling an object or appliance. Later, an audible event occurs in the continuing scene. The assistant must detect the right event, recall the armed instruction, and speak without another user prompt.

Implicit: while putting bread into a toaster, “Let me know when this is ready.”

Explicit: “This should make a sound when it’s done—let me know.”

Out of view: while setting down a phone, “Let me know if this rings.” The wearer walks away before the ring.

The video and native scene audio continue throughout. The injected cue is a separate, logged audio stem. A cue may arrive while the assistant is silent or while it is speaking. Only the speaking condition tests model-initiated interruption; the silent condition tests proactive alerting.

2. What each condition establishes

conditionrequired evidenceprimary capability
Explicit triggerThe prompt states the expected sound or event.Prospective-memory retention, audio detection, timing, and response control. This is the clean control.
Implicit triggerThe prompt uses a visually grounded referent or outcome; the cue class is inferred from the visible setup.Visual grounding, event inference, prospective memory, and sound detection.
Out of viewThe source is verified absent or fully occluded through the cue interval.Longer-lived binding between the armed instruction and its source.
Assistant speakingThe cue begins inside verified assistant speech and the runtime continues listening while generating.External-event detection plus self-preemption and repair.

Out-of-view footage strengthens the memory requirement. It is cross-modal only when information needed to arm or resolve the reminder came from vision. A prompt that names the phone and its ringtone can be solved from language plus audio, so it remains valuable as a control rather than the hardest cross-modal item.

Capability-label rule: if audio plus the prompt transcript uniquely identifies when and why to fire, label the item condition_following_control. Use visual_grounding or cross_modal_binding only when a visual-only referent, setup fact, source selection, or payload is necessary. Confirm this with a video-masked ablation.

4. Version-1 scope

Version 1 admits microwave completion, toaster pop, electric-kettle completion, and phone ringing. A conventional oven or laundry cycle is admitted only when the source footage contains a plausible elapsed interval, a visible timer/preheat state, or an explicit time discontinuity. A thirty-second synthetic “washer finished” event does not ship.

5. Reminder lifecycle

Each item follows one state path:

UNARMED → ARMING → ARMED → TARGET_CUE_IDENTIFIABLE → FIRED → QUIESCENT

state boundaryoperational definition
arm_start_sFirst sample of the wearer’s reminder request.
arm_end_sEnd of the request. The target binding and payload must already be recoverable.
activation_complete_sThe appliance is visibly started or the phone is visibly placed. It may occur just after the arm request when speech accompanies the setup.
armed_smax(arm_end_s, activation_complete_s). The target cue may qualify only after this point.
cue_onset_sFirst non-silent sample of the injected cue stem.
cue_identifiable_sFirst moment a human auditor can identify the target cue class. Timing scores use this point.
cue_offset_sLast non-silent cue sample.
response_onset_sFirst audible token that begins a relevant reminder response.

The cue must be the first qualifying target event after arming and cue_identifiable_s > armed_s. Version-1 items require at least 5 seconds between armed_s and cue_identifiable_s. Results are stratified into 5–15 s, 15–60 s, and over-60 s memory gaps.

6. Deictic and implicit grounding

“This” is permitted here because it can bind to an object at the moment the reminder is armed. It is not a license for an unanchored later recall question.

  1. The complete word span for this, that, or it must overlap frames where the target is visible.
  2. Hand contact, placement, operation, or an unambiguous pointing gesture must select the target.
  3. No competing object may fit the same deictic phrase at that moment.
  4. The visible setup must support a conventional completion event. “Let me know when this is ready” is rejected if nothing is visibly started.
  5. The target and setup are checked in pixels. Narration text may propose them but cannot certify them.

The implicit condition must remove the textual shortcut. For example, “Let me know when this is ready” during microwave activation requires vision to infer the relevant source and likely completion cue. “The microwave will beep; remind me when it does” belongs to the explicit control.

Two reviewers independently judge implicit intent with the cue identity hidden. They must agree on the referent, completed activation, and conventional expected cue from the visible setup. Disagreement quarantines the item.

7. Cue construction

  • Natural duration: preserve the sound class. A phone may ring for 2–3 s; a toaster pop may last less than half a second. Sound length is not the scoring window.
  • Plausible time: place the cue near a natural completion, return, check, or retrieval when the footage supplies one. Do not make an oven or washer finish implausibly quickly.
  • Separate stem: retain native scene, wearer, assistant, and cue stems separately. Record asset hash, sample rate, gain, channel map, onset, and mix command.
  • Acoustic consistency: match loudness, distance, room response, and occlusion. A phone in another room should sound farther away than a phone beside the glasses microphone.
  • Asset diversity: use several independently sourced or synthesized exemplars per cue class. Split cue assets and source parents across evaluation partitions.
  • Collision gate: the first pilot rejects native speech or another salient sound over the cue-identification interval. A later hard arm introduces controlled overlap.
  • Blind audibility gate: on the final mix, at least the frozen reviewer threshold must identify the cue class without seeing its label. The mix must introduce no click, gain step, or channel artifact that reveals the variant.

Cue placement is counterfactual, but the visible trajectory should remain credible. A completion cue immediately before the wearer naturally returns to the microwave is stronger than an arbitrary beep while unrelated activity continues.

A kettle never receives a generic synthetic beep merely because it was switched on. Some electric kettles make a small automatic-shutoff click, whistling stovetop kettles whistle, and other kettles provide no distinct completion cue. The device type and final sound must pass the blind identity gate or the candidate is rejected.

9. Silent and duplex variants

assistant state at cuerequired responseadditional score
silentInitiate the reminder without a new user turn.Reminder latency.
speakingStop the current answer, issue the reminder, and abandon any obsolete partial clause.Preemption latency and obsolete-speech overrun.

The two variants use the same base scene, cue asset, cue time, and reminder content. The runtime must ingest scene audio while generating output and expose timestamps for both final assistant audio and cancellation. If it cannot, the duplex arm is unsupported rather than silently approximated.

10. Timing and scoring

The pilot uses a provisional 3.0 s response deadline measured from cue_identifiable_s, a provisional 2.0 s late grace interval, and a provisional 100 ms clock tolerance. observation_end_s is the grace end. All four values are frozen only after measuring the evaluation stack’s audio-ingest, decoding, TTS, and timestamp latency; none is tuned per model.

outcomerule for first relevant audible token
early false alarmresponse_onset_s < cue_onset_s − clock_tolerance_s
onset-to-identifiable reactionA response beginning after acoustic onset but before the human-identifiable point is retained and inspected; it is not automatically called early.
on timecue_identifiable_s ≤ response_onset_s ≤ deadline_s
lateAfter the deadline but within the frozen grace interval
missedNo qualifying reminder within the observation interval

Report continuous latency as well as bands. Trigger timing and reminder content are separate dimensions:

  • Trigger recall: target variants that produce a timely reminder.
  • False-alarm rate: wrong-class, no-cue, pre-arm, repeat, and unarmed controls that produce an invalid reminder.
  • Binding accuracy: the response names the correct source, state, or stored payload. “I heard something” is detection evidence but not a successful reminder.
  • Fire-once accuracy: one valid response per armed one-shot obligation.
  • Duplex preemption: time from identifiable cue to termination of the interrupted assistant clause.

Recall is never reported without the matched false-alarm rate.

13. Construction pipeline

  1. Mine setups: retrieve insert, load, place, activate, and leave-view proposals from action metadata.
  2. Verify vision: review coarse frames, then dense frames around activation, deictic speech, departure, cue, and any natural retrieval.
  3. Audit native audio: mark speech and salient sound intervals; reject accidental target-like cues in controls.
  4. Choose a plausible cue time: use the natural trajectory and preserve a usable response interval.
  5. Author and align prompts: synthesize natural speech, then validate word timing and deictic overlap.
  6. Build cue stems: normalize and spatialize approved assets; retain hashes and mix metadata.
  7. Render the counterfactual group: target, wrong, none, pre-arm, repeat, and unarmed variants.
  8. Add duplex pairs: make assistant-silent and assistant-speaking versions from the same source plan.
  9. Run deterministic gates: clocks, overlaps, hashes, variant invariants, cue audibility, and response runway.
  10. Audit independently: two reviewers verify setup, binding, plausibility, cue identity, cue timing, and expected response. Resolve every disagreement before release.

15. Pilot decision

Proceed with a small local pilot using toaster and phone proposals, plus kettle scenes only when their actual device class supports a recognizable end sound. Keep microwave scenes at the front of the retrieval queue because their metadata shows the clearest insertion, control, departure, and retrieval trajectories, pending media and native-audio verification. Hold laundry and conventional oven items until a plausible full cycle or timer-based event is available.

The pilot should validate four things before scaling: whether implicit prompts are visually unambiguous, whether injected cues remain natural in the glasses microphone mix, whether a runtime can hear while speaking, and whether the 3 s provisional deadline is compatible with measured end-to-end latency.
