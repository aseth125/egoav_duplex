Action-episodic memory questions — end-to-end design

Status: 18 questions shipped from 8 published episodes (2026-10-04). Everything below is what actually ran, with the numbers it produced. Where a step failed, the failure is written down rather than the repaired version only — the failures are the reason most of the gates exist.

Run the whole thing: bash duplex/avd/run_em_gates.sh → EM_CHAIN_OK (13 steps, producers before consumers, stops at the first non-OK token).

1. What the question is for

The wearer asks the assistant what they themselves were doing a moment ago:

"And before I pushed the bathroom door open, what was I on?"

To answer, the model has to hold a running record of the wearer's own actions and index into it by an event, not by a clock. That is the skill. Three things have to be true or the question measures something else:

  1. The anchor must have already happened at the moment of asking. If the wearer is still walking into the bathroom, "before I walked into the bathroom" is not a memory cue — it is a prediction.
  2. The anchor must not be the answer. If the question names the thing the answer has to say, there is nothing to recall.
  3. The window between anchor and ask must contain something to recall. A window where the wearer did one thing, slowly, is not an episodic-memory item; it is a caption.

Every gate in this pipeline enforces one of those three.

2. The unit is a clause, not a row

Narration arrives as overlapping ~5 s rows, each a multi-clause summary, and each row's last clause is the next row's first. Treating a row as an event double-counts, and treating the whole window as one event erases the structure the question depends on.

So rows are split into clauses, and clauses are merged into runs when they share both a head object and an action verb (jaccard ≥ 0.34). The threshold is deliberately strict: gap_coverage — how much of the anchor→ask window the merged runs actually account for — is computed from it, and a loose merge would inflate that number by swallowing unrelated clauses.

At answer level, adjacent runs sharing a canonical head collapse into one item. A run shorter than INTERRUPT_MAX (2.5 s) wedged between two runs with the same head is a break: it gets folded into the surrounding item and named rather than listed, because "you were doing the drawers, you paused to move the remote, you went back to the drawers" is one memory with a hiccup, not three memories.

Current scale: 31 candidate turns → 27 merged runs → 18 answer-level item sets. Median answer 23 words; 11 answers have 1 item, 5 have 2, 2 have 3. gap_coverage median 0.76, minimum 0.12.

4. Choosing the anchor

A proposal from narration, then four ways to lose it.

Deixis rule. Narration proposes the anchor → vision confirms it by object persistence across head turns → if vision contradicts narration, vision names the activity itself → the anchor is dropped only when vision has nothing nameable. The anchor may never be the activity the question is asking for.

Statuses on the 31 candidates:

18 shipped of 31 = 58%. That rate is the planning number for scale-up, and it is reported on the page rather than hidden.

5. The leak gate — why the question may not name the answer

item_leak scans the question for any word naming something the answer has to utter. Three widenings, each forced by a real escape:

  1. **Items only → items and their folded breaks.** The TV turn's anchor was "before I set the remote down", in front of an answer whose break is the one-second gesture with that same remote. A thing the answer must say is a thing the question may not say, whether it is a whole item or two words inside one.
  2. **Head words only → head words and object words in the evidence text.** One anchor said "mug" while the item's evidence read "puts the spoon down in the mug" — head spoon, so the head check saw nothing.
  3. A PLACE exemption, applied asymmetrically. Widening (2) immediately flagged 9 surfaces — counter, wall, table, closet. Where an action happened is scenery, and naming the scenery is the anchor's whole job: *"before I set the mug down on the counter"* says nothing about a tea bag soaking. So places are exempt from the evidence scan — but not from the head check, because when a place is the item's own head the answer is about that place and it leaks like anything else.

A fourth fix was in the vocabulary, not the gate: "mug" was not a known object word at all, which is why both mug leaks were invisible to every version of the scan.

6. Temporal alignment — two checks, and vision wins

This is the owner's requirement: the exit-bathroom anchor cannot come before the person is exiting the bathroom. Two independent checks stand behind it.

Narration side (em_anchor_timing) searches the episode's own action list for the anchor's nouns. NOT_YET if the first mention lands after the ask, STALE if the last lands more than 12 s before it. Cheap, runs over everything, and wrong often enough that it cannot be the decider: the narrator names an object when it becomes the subject of a sentence, not when it enters frame.

Vision side (em_vision_v1) is the decider. Every shipped anchor has a contact strip cut around its ask and a written reading stored in the table — a sentence about pixels at named timestamps, not a tick.

verdictnmeaning
confirmed16the strip shows the event finished or underway at the ask
weak1the action is visible, the object's identity rests on narration
unconfirmed1the frames neither show nor contradict it — held, not dropped
refuted0hard gate failure if it ever appears

Four anchors earned refuted during the pass. None are in the table: they were rewritten or held out, and what the frames said is recorded in the entry that replaced them.

Final timing tally: 18 checked — 12 ok, 4 vision_only, 2 overturned by frames (T5_at_390 p01 NOT_YET, T5_at_120 p06 STALE), 0 failures. Both narration-side failures were lag.

The downgrade is an escape hatch, so it has a fixture pair: readings present → EAT_OK, vision table empty → EAT_FAIL. It can only be silenced by someone who has looked at the strip and written down what is in it.

6.1 Contact strips, and the trap in them

4-tile hstack from the published 720×720 transcode, ASK tile stamped green. Default offsets ask−6 / −3 / 0 / +3; EAF_OFFS / EAF_ONLY / EAF_TAG override. Four of 18 needed something else: three too tight for 3 s steps (a remote set down, a crouch starting, a door swinging), one too wide.

A strip that is too narrow does not report "cannot see it" — it reports the wrong answer.

Proved on T5_at_120 p15: at ±6 s all four tiles look down on the same coffee table from apparently the same place, and the default strip said the walk-round never happened. Widened to ±14 s it is unmistakable — the sofa swings from the top edge to the left edge and the whole layout rotates.

6.2 The owner's own example

T10_at_600 p04 was asked mid-threshold. Fine strip: bathroom door shut at 78.7 s, swinging at 79.5 s, open at the 80.2 s ask, interior not resolved until 80.9 s. So the walk-in is mid-stride at the ask. Re-anchored to the door push, which is behind her:

"Hang on, take it back one more — what was I on before I pushed this door open?"

7. Both answers are kept

The owner asked to keep both. In the emitted spec each turn carries:

  • text — the anchored long question (the ship style)
  • question_short_unused — the short style, kept as a fallback if a line reads

heavy aloud

  • question_v53 — the question it replaced
  • expected_answer.text — the composed, item-listing answer
  • expected_answer.text_v53 — the previous gold, kept beside it
  • anchor — seen_at_ask, anchor_long, the vision verdict, the reading,

and the strip path

Turns the pass could not use are left exactly as they were and listed in held_out on the chain. Dropping them would shift every later turn's timing.

8. Two numbering traps (both caught by guards, both real)

Position in em_runs and idx in a chain are different numbering schemes.

  • Matching on position rewrote the wrong turn in two chains. The guard

fired on 4 turns; without it, four plausible-but-wrong questions would have shipped silently.

  • Matching on text then over-corrected. The string stored as

question_v53 is in fact the v51 text — the deixis pass rewrote 9 of 19 questions in one chain and 12 of 19 in the other — so "newest spec that still contains these strings" resolved all six chains down to v51 and would have quietly discarded every deixis repair. Passing is not the same as correct.

Resolution: two-legged lookup. Text locates the turn in v51, where that text actually lives; the turn's t_s — identical across specs, and the only field that is — carries it into the newest base. Text for identity, time for the join. Fixture pair: a base holding every t_s is chosen, a base missing one is refused.

9. Rendering the listening set

  • Never CSM. renderers.check raises on it. Breeze (BreezeTTS-2) is the

renderer; measured median 3.29 words/s.

  • Slot feasibility is checked before rendering, not discovered after. For

each turn, time to the next onset minus the 0.4 s handoff versus words/3.29. All 18 long questions fit; tightest margin 1.3 s.

  • Whole chains, not 18 turns. The published audio is specs_v50; these

questions are built on v51/v53_4, authored later and never rendered. Those generations do not share turn positions, and the wav key is u-NN by position — so for these chains every id on disk already points at different text. Dropping 18 wavs into that set would place them beside audio for questions the spec no longer contains.

  • New wav root (wavs_v54). Same reason from the other side: the ids

collide with the v50 release's own wavs, and rendering into wavs_v2 would overwrite published audio.

  • Voice pinned to 8. Voice number is "index of this participant among all

participants, corpus-wide" — correct as a rule, but the corpus shrank 498 → 477 chains between generations and james_johnson slid from slot 8 to slot 7. Slot 7 is a different speaker. The participant has not changed; only the denominator has. Pinned in pin_voice_v54.py so the next rebuild cannot silently undo it.

  • Assistant turns are never synthesised. Text lane only. Speaking the gold

answer into the audio track would feed the model the thing it is being tested on.

10. Scaling to the rest (469 episodes, ~1447 turns)

Not started. The shape it has to take, given what the 8-episode sample showed:

  1. Expect ~58% yield. Budget for it; do not treat rejected windows as a bug to be fixed away.
  2. Reject degenerate windows up front. ~26% of windows on this sample had nothing distinct to recall. An action-density floor at window-selection time is cheaper than authoring a question and throwing it out.
  3. The vision step is the expensive part and cannot be skipped. Hand-written readings do not scale to 1447 turns, so it becomes a VLM multiple-choice step — Gemma-31B via transformers (not vLLM), one H200, sharded on h200_usr-sr_high. Multiple-choice, because free-text verdicts cannot be gated.
  4. Set the agreement floor BEFORE the run. A stratified self-audit whose threshold is chosen after seeing the numbers measures nothing.
  5. Templated anchors, not authored ones — but every template still has to pass the same leak gate and the same timing gate. The gates are the transferable part of this work; the 18 questions are not.

11. Visual verification

Every family in this project now carries this section, and this one carries it first because this family is where the rule was learned. §6 is the evidence; this is the rule stated so it can be checked.

Narration is a proposal. Pixels decide. The narrator names an object when it becomes the subject of a sentence, not when it enters frame, so narration-side timing was wrong often enough that it cannot be the decider — 2 of 18 shipped anchors were overturned by frames, and 4 more earned refuted and never reached the table.

What a shipped item must have:

  1. A frame-level verdict per anchor, one of confirmed / weak / unconfirmed / refuted. refuted is a hard failure. unconfirmed is held, not dropped — "the frames do not settle it" is a real state and collapsing it into either neighbour is a lie in one direction.
  2. A written reading, not a tick. A sentence about pixels at named timestamps. A boolean verdict can be produced by a gate that never opened an image; a reading that names what is at 79.5 s cannot.
  3. A strip wide enough to contain the event. §6.1: a strip that is too narrow does not report cannot see it, it reports the wrong answer. T5_at_120 p15 said the walk-round never happened at ±6 s and was unmistakable at ±14 s. Widening is part of the check, not a repair.
  4. Vision overrides narration when they disagree, and the disagreement is written into the entry rather than erased by the rewrite.

At 1,447 turns the hand-written reading does not scale, so it becomes a multiple-choice VLM pass (Gemma-31B via transformers, one H200, sharded on h200_usr-sr_high) — multiple-choice because a free-text verdict cannot be gated. The agreement floor against a human sample is set before the run, not after seeing the numbers.

12. Checklist

  • [ ] ACTMEM-02 The anchor is not the activity the answer has to name.
  • [ ] ACTMEM-03 The anchor→ask window contains something distinct to recall (gap_coverage recorded, degenerate windows rejected).
  • [ ] ACTMEM-04 The leak gate ran over items and their folded breaks, over head words and evidence object words, with the PLACE exemption applied to evidence only.
  • [ ] ACTMEM-05 Every object word in the question is in the known-object vocabulary (the "mug" hole), so the scan could see it.
  • [ ] ACTMEM-06 The item carries a frame-level vision verdict and a written reading, per §11.
  • [ ] ACTMEM-07 No item ships on refuted; unconfirmed items are held, not quietly dropped.
  • [ ] ACTMEM-08 The strip was widened wherever the default offsets could not contain the event.

Hard rules carried in

  • Never render with CSM.
  • sam31_c015/ and tranche */sam31/ are read-only; never delete a SAM3 mask.
  • Never delete anything — quarantine with a written reason.
  • Heavy work through srun/sbatch, never the login node.
  • Cap image-reading agents at 40 frames.
  • Verify agent claims against disk before recording them as fact.
  • A guard needs a fixture pair — one case that must trip it, one that must

not. A test that only runs the real data passes equally well if the guard fires unconditionally.

  • Beware the asserted-field gate: a field written by a counter or a hash and

then validated by a gate reading that same field proves nothing. Eleven of those turned up in one night. Honest values are always worse first.
