Action episodic memory — before and after

8 published episodes · 31 recall turns · 23 usable · one clip per turn

The problem. An answer must not read “you were browsing the TV menu, then browsing the TV menu, then browsing the TV menu”. One activity gets reported once, with how long it lasted.
Why it could not be fixed by merging narration rows. A row is not one action. It carries one to three clauses, and its LAST clause is the FIRST clause of the next row — three events written as three overlapping pairs. Row 1 against row 2 is (A,B) against (B,C): genuinely different, so no threshold collapses them, and lowering it until they do collapse merges A with C as well. The unit of a memory is the clause, so the clauses come out first and the merge runs over those.
Across the 23 usable windows: 44 narration rows → 92 clauses → 49 transitions dropped → 31 things actually done.
How to read a card. Watch the clip — it is cut to the window and the band across the top says which phase is on screen. Then the question as it reads today, and under it the two anchored rewrites. Then the answer as it reads today, and under it the answer it should give. Under that is the list of things the answer is checked against: every one of them has to be named, in order, or the turn does not ship.
COVERAGE is how much of the window the listed activities actually fill. A window at 0.12 is thirty seconds of sitting still with one stray gesture in it, and the honest answer there is “you were just sitting there” — not the gesture reported as if it were the whole window.
