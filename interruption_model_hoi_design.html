interruption_model_hoi — the model has to speak first

Status: design only (2026-10-05). Nothing is built. The counts below are measured off briefs_v4 and the Nymeria tracks tonight; the design is not.

Run the whole thing (once it exists): bash duplex/avd/run_hoi_gates.sh → HOI_CHAIN_OK.

Family string: interruption_model_hoi.

Sisters: docs/EPISODIC_MEMORY_DESIGN.md (action memory), docs/OBJECT_EPISODIC_MEMORY_DESIGN.md (object memory). This document borrows most of its machinery from the object one and inverts the time axis.

1. What this family is for

The wearer leaves a standing instruction, then forgets about it. Later the thing happens, and the model has to cut in on its own.

"Remind me when I pick up my keys." … 90 seconds of unrelated activity … "You've just picked up the keys."

Every other family in EgoDuplex answers. This is the only one where the model has to start a turn. That is the whole point of it, and it is why the family is named for the interruption rather than for the reminder.

Three properties make it a real test:

  1. The trigger is in the future at instruction time. The model cannot answer from what it has already seen; it has to keep watching.
  2. The scored thing is a moment, not a sentence. Speaking the right words at the wrong second is a failure.
  3. Sometimes the trigger never comes, and the correct behaviour is to say nothing for the rest of the episode.

Property 3 is the one that stops the family being free. Without it a model that fires every ten seconds is perfect.

2. What it borrows, and what is new

Borrowed from the object doc, unchanged: the HANDLE verb extraction, head-noun keying, the NOT_OBJECT filter, the briefs_v4 / specs_v51 plumbing, the control-arm rule, the two-prompt SAM3 finding, and the fixture-pair convention for every guard.

New here:

  • the trigger ladder (§3) — several kinds of standing instruction, not one
  • model interruption — the model speaks over the wearer (§6)
  • scoring on time, with four outcomes (§7)
  • the seated gate on the generic arm (§8)
  • negatives, which are built last and are the hard half (§9)

3.1 The T3 count, and the mistake that nearly halved it

vis on a track frame looks like a visibility probability and is not. Real values run from 2e-05 to about 0.02, and vis is never zero. Thresholding at vis > 0.5 returned 14 reappearance events corpus-wide and would have killed the arm as too small.

4. Where the triggers come from

T1, pick up

Narration, PICK verbs — picks up, grabs, takes, lifts, retrieves, collects, reaches for — plus an article and a noun, keyed on head noun, filtered through NOT_OBJECT.

pick events in narration3,085
first pick of a given object1,494
far enough in to site an instruction before it1,167
median picks per clip2

The first qualifying pick after the instruction is the answer. Not any pick. A model that stays quiet through pick one and fires on pick two has missed, and must be scored as a miss, not a late hit.

T2, put down

The PLACE frame already built for object memory: verb + article + object + preposition + destination, all four. 2,575 events. A bare verb alternation gives 12,508 by reading "turns left" as "left it somewhere".

T3, see / appears

Track frame-gap, as in §3.1. Free ground truth.

T4, nth time

Objects picked repeatedly inside one clip: 586 picked twice or more, 307 three times or more. The instruction names the ordinal, so the model has to count, not just detect. Cheap, and it tests something none of the other arms do.

5. What the instruction looks like

Spoken, short, and it must not describe the trigger well enough to be answered without watching.

Good:

"Remind me when I pick up my keys." "I'm looking for a chair — tell me if you see one." "Shout when I put the knife down." "Tell me the third time I go into the fridge."

Not acceptable:

  • "Remind me when I pick up the knife to start chopping." — "chopping"

leaks it. The model fires on the narration of the next activity and never needs to see a knife. Leak rule: the instruction may name the object and the action, and nothing else about the future.

  • "Remind me when I pick that up." — bare pointer, no referent. This

family names its object, like object memory and unlike everything else.

  • "Remind me in two minutes." — a timer, not an observation.

5.1 The instruction carries a payload

"Remind me to call mum when I pick up my phone."

This is how people actually talk, it costs nothing to build, and it makes the interruption scorable on content as well as timing. A model that fires at the right second and has forgotten what the reminder was for has only done half the job.

Payload is optional per item — some instructions are bare ("just tell me when"). Target mix is roughly half and half, so that a model cannot learn that a reminder always has a payload.

5.2 Wording must vary

No fixed template. Each instruction is paraphrased with the intent preserved: remind me / tell me / let me know / shout when / give me a nudge when / don't let me forget. Register varies too — some are polite, some are clipped mid-task.

A guard counts distinct surface forms per trigger kind and fails the build if any single phrasing is more than a fifth of its arm. Diversity that is asserted rather than measured is not diversity.

6. What the interruption looks like

Three parts. The first two are required.

"Heads up — you've just picked up the keys." "That's the chair you were after, on your right." "Keys in hand — you wanted to call your mum."

  1. A cut-in opener. Short, because the wearer is probably mid-something.
  2. The trigger, named. The object and what just happened — this is the evidence the model is claiming, and if it is wrong the item is wrong even when the timing was right.
  3. The payload, when the instruction had one.

Not acceptable: reciting the instruction back ("you asked me to remind you when you picked up your keys, and you have now picked up your keys"), a timestamp, or any reference to anything after the trigger moment.

6.1 Model interruption — it speaks over the wearer

The owner's call: the model speaks at the trigger, even if the wearer is mid-sentence. It does not wait for a gap.

The reason is that waiting is indistinguishable from being late, and the whole family is a latency test. A model allowed to wait for silence can sit on every trigger until the wearer stops talking and never be caught.

This is representable today — turns already carry an interruption field — but the cases here are the first where the model is the interrupter rather than the wearer, so the renderer and the scorer both need to be taught that a model-initiated barge-in is legal. That is new work, not configuration.

7. Scoring

Four outcomes, and only one of them is good:

outcomemeaning
hitspeaks inside the tolerance window around the trigger
earlyspeaks before the window — it guessed, it did not observe
latespeaks after the window
missnever speaks on a positive
false alarmspeaks on a negative

Report hit rate and false-alarm rate together, always. Either alone is trivially gamed in opposite directions, and a family that reports only hit rate is a family that rewards a model which never stops talking.

7.1 The tolerance window is not yet a number

The trigger time comes from narration action spans, which are coarse. A window tighter than the annotation noise scores noise; a window looser than the event makes the task free.

So the window is measured, not chosen: take a sample of T1 triggers, tighten each one against vision (object plus hand in frame), and look at how far the vision moment sits from the narration moment. The window is set from that spread. Provisionally [-1 s, +5 s] — asymmetric because speaking early is a different failure from speaking late — but that pair of numbers is a placeholder and must not ship unmeasured.

8. The generic arm, and the seated gate

"Tell me if I pick anything up" needs the first pick of anything to be correct. One pick missing from the narration turns a correct silence into a scored miss, and narration is sparse. Across the whole corpus that risk is unacceptable.

The owner's fix: allow the generic arm only where the wearer is sitting still. Sitting still means few actions, a small reachable set, and narration that is far more likely to have caught everything.

Measured: 144 of 477 clips have a seated stretch of 60 s or more, median 174 s. That is a comfortable amount of material for a small arm.

The gate needs two independent signals that agree, because either alone is weak:

  1. Narration text — a seated cue (*sits, seated, at the table, on the couch) with no ambulatory cue (walks, steps, stairs, heads into*) in the same span. This is what the 144 was measured with.
  2. Camera motion — frame-to-frame difference on the cached 2 fps frames. An ego camera on a walking person moves enormously; on a seated person it barely moves. This signal is independent of the narration, which is the point of having it.

Where they disagree the clip is not used. No generic item ships on one signal.

9. Negatives — built last, and not by truncation

The owner's call: do not truncate episodes. So negatives come from the object route.

A negative is an instruction whose trigger never fires for the rest of the episode, where the correct behaviour is silence throughout.

objects handled but never picked, with ≥60 s of clip left2,977
clips offering at least one425 of 477

Target share 10–20% of the family, per the owner.

Three routes, in order of how much they can be trusted:

  1. Truncate the episode before a known trigger. Airtight, free — and ruled out, because it changes the clip boundary.
  2. Name an object the narration never picks (the 2,977). Cheap, and the route being taken. Its weakness is exactly the weakness of absence evidence everywhere in this project: narration silence is not proof. An unnarrated pick turns correct silence into a scored miss, and it would do so silently.
  3. Visual sweep of the whole remaining clip. Correct, expensive.

So route 2 ships with route 3 as its gate: for every negative, a visual pass over the remaining clip must fail to find the object in the wearer's hands. That is the same control discipline as object memory — a negative nobody checked is not a negative, it is an unexamined claim.

This is why negatives are built last: they are the expensive half, and the positives are useful on their own while the sweep is being built.

10. Visual verification

Every trigger in §3 is currently proposed by text or by tracks. Neither is a pixel. This family scores a time, so an unverified trigger time is not a soft weakness — it is the answer key being wrong by seconds.

What is proposed, and what has to confirm it:

triggerproposed bywhat vision must settle
T1 pick upnarration verbthe frame where the hand first holds it
T2 put downnarration verbthe frame where contact is released
T3 see / appearstrack t_s gapsthat the object is genuinely visible at the returning frame
T4 nth timecounted occurrencesevery occurrence, not just the nth
T5 genericnarration + seated gatethat the wearer is seated and the pick is real

Four rules:

  1. **The fire time is tightened at the frame, never taken raw from narration.** The narrator names an object when it becomes the subject of a sentence. In the sister family that lag overturned 2 of 18 anchors. Here it lands directly in the scored tolerance window (§7.1), so the window must be measured after tightening, not before.
  2. T3 is tracks only today, and tracks are a projection. §3.1 is the warning: one misread of vis moved the count from 14 to 10,747. The projection has never been checked against pixels in this family. A "clean return" ships only when a frame shows the object, and the per-object median-vis floor is a filter, not evidence.
  3. Absence claims need every frame, not a sample. The negatives arm (§9) asserts the object is never handled in the remaining window. A sampled sweep that misses one pick turns a negative into a mislabelled positive — the single worst row this family can emit. The detector also needs a control: it must be shown finding the object where the object is known to be present, or its silence means nothing.
  4. Disagreement is quarantined, with the disagreement written down. Where narration says pick and the frames show the hand passing over, the item does not ship and the reading is kept. Deleting it loses the one example that would have taught the gate.

Instruction time is also a visual fact. HOI-03 — the object is not already in hand — cannot be decided from the verb list; it is a question about a frame, and it is checked at one.

Cost. Two passes, as in the object family: coarse 2 fps to locate, dense 15 fps around the candidate instant. Detector is SAM3 with a Qwen3-Omni-30B spot-check, because a single detector's miss is indistinguishable from an absence.

  • [ ] HOI-03 The object is not already in hand at instruction time — otherwise the first trigger is ambiguous.
  • [ ] HOI-04 The scored trigger is the first qualifying one after the instruction.

13. Open, and what is still unknown

  1. The tolerance window (§7.1). Everything downstream depends on it and it has not been measured.
  2. T6 walk away without and T7 duration are unmeasured and unscoped.
  3. The renderer and scorer do not yet understand a model-initiated barge-in (§6.1).
  4. Whether the T3 "clean return" rule — per-object median vis — matches what a person would call seeing the thing. It is a reasonable rule and it is untested against a human. A small hand audit should settle it before 10,747 items are built on top of it.
