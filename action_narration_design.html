action_narration — "what am I doing right now?"

Status: design only (2026-10-05). Nothing built. Counts below are measured off briefs_v4 tonight; the design is not.

Run the whole thing (once it exists): bash duplex/avd/run_actnar_gates.sh → ACTNAR_CHAIN_OK.

Family string: action_narration.

Sister document: docs/DURATION_ACTIVITY_DESIGN.md. The two share one component — the LLM activity segmenter — which is specified there, in full, once. This document consumes it.

1. What the question is for

The wearer asks the model what they are doing.

"Hey — what am I doing right now?" "You're making a cup of tea."

It sounds trivial and in its naive form it is (§6). What makes it worth building is that it forces a decision no other family forces: at what level of description does the model answer, and as of which second.

2. The data

clips with narration477
action spans34,208
span length5.0 s, uniform
narration coverage of the clip~100%, contiguous
spans with no posture word12.3%

Ground truth for something is therefore free. Ground truth for the right thing is not — see §4.

2.1 121 broken spans, quarantine before use

121 action spans have an end at or before their start, including negative times: [2.2, -0.3], [403.5, 400.7]. Both this family and duration_activity index off this grid.

They are quarantined with the reason written down, never deleted. A builder that silently skips a malformed span produces a clip with a hole in its timeline and no record that the hole is there.

3. When "now" is — anchor at the end of the question

The question takes about two seconds to say. The activity can change inside those two seconds. So "now" has to be defined, and the owner's instinct is to count from where the question ends.

That is right, for two reasons: it is the first moment the model has heard the whole question, and it is the only anchor that is causal. Anchoring at the question's start asks the model to answer using a second it had not finished listening through.

And it matters more than it looks. Measured over every adjacent pair of 5-second spans in the corpus:

median content-word overlap between neighbouring spans0.44
pairs sharing less than half their content words55% (18,515 of 33,731)

The description genuinely changes every few seconds. Picking the wrong 5-second block really does change the answer.

3.1 The obvious guard is vacuous, so use a different one

The instinct is "don't place a question near a span boundary". The spans are a uniform 5 s grid, so every possible anchor is within 2.5 s of a boundary. That guard rejects nothing, or everything, depending on the threshold — and either way it is not doing what it says.

The guard that works is on the answer side:

An answer is correct if it matches the span containing the question end or the span immediately before it.

Those two are genuinely ambiguous to a person too. A model is not wrong for describing the thing that was happening one second earlier.

Placement is still rejected in one case: when the question end lands within 1 s of a boundary and the two neighbouring spans describe different activities. There the item has no defensible single answer and should not exist.

4. The level of description — the central problem

Narration is micro-motion:

"C is tiptoeing with both her feet while standing by the kitchen countertop as she pushes a jar container in the cupboard…"

A person asked what they are doing says "making tea." Three levels are available and only one of them is the answer:

levelexamplesource
micro-motion"pushing a jar into the cupboard"narration, verbatim
activity"making a cup of tea"LLM segmenter
purpose"getting breakfast ready before work"inferred, often unknowable

Ship the activity level. Micro-motion is what the data says and not what anybody asks for; purpose is frequently not recoverable from the clip and would be invented.

This is the whole reason the LLM segmenter exists, and it is the main cost of this family. It is specified in docs/DURATION_ACTIVITY_DESIGN.md §4.

4.1 A failure worth recording: word-overlap segmentation does not work

Before reaching for an LLM, activity segments were derived cheaply, by merging neighbouring spans that shared content words. It fails, and it is worth knowing how it fails.

The themes it produced are posture word-soup, not activities:

arms clasped lap leans forward backrest couch living floor pillow

One genuine activity did surface (arcade button chair game joystick), but the common theme words across the whole run were living, forward, holding, area, leans, resting, arms, bends — body-pose vocabulary, because that is what the narration is mostly made of. Stripping a posture stop-list first did not save it.

At three merge thresholds the method gave 588–1,184 "meaningful" segments with a median around 67 s. Those numbers are not an estimate of what the LLM segmenter will produce; they are the measurements of a method that was rejected. They are recorded so nobody re-derives them and thinks the problem is solved.

5. What the question and answer look like

Question — short, spoken, no anchor, no object named:

"What am I doing right now?" "Hey, remind me what I'm in the middle of." "What's going on — what am I doing?"

Wording varies, intent preserved. A guard fails the build if any single phrasing exceeds a fifth of the family.

Answer — second person, one clause, present continuous:

"You're making a cup of tea." "You're folding laundry on the landing."

Rules:

  • Second person. The narration is third person about "C" and must be

turned around. A key that says "C is washing a plate" is not an answer to "what am I doing".

  • One activity. Narration often chains with while. Where two are

genuinely concurrent, the key holds both as an accepted set and either scores.

  • No timestamp, no duration. Duration belongs to the sister family.
  • Nothing after the question end. The same no-future rule as the memory

families.

6. Triviality, and the three hard conditions

A single frame at the question moment answers most of these. That makes the plain arm a captioning task wearing a duplex task's clothes. It is still worth having — as a floor that calibrates the harder families — but it should be labelled as a floor and not counted as evidence of anything else.

Three conditions make items that are not solvable from one frame. Each becomes a flag on the item so the two populations can be scored apart:

  1. hands_offscreen — the manipulation is out of view at the anchor, so the activity is only recoverable from what came before.
  2. audio_only — the activity is identified by sound (a kettle, a tap, a conversation) rather than by what is visible.
  3. purpose — the question asks why rather than what. Only built where the narration or speech licenses the purpose; otherwise it is invention.

Expect these to cut the size hard. That is the correct trade: a small arm that measures something beats a large arm that measures a captioner.

7. Visual verification

The answer in this family is produced without a frame ever being opened. The segmenter reads narration text (DURATION_ACTIVITY_DESIGN.md §4.4), the anchor is an index into a text grid, and the accepted key is two text spans. That is the gap this section closes, and it is a larger gap here than in the memory families, because there the narration at least proposed something a strip was then cut around.

Every shipped answer is confirmed at the anchor frame. The activity named in the key must be the activity visible at the second the question ends. A mismatch is not softened into weak; the item does not ship and the reading is kept.

7.1 The three hard flags are visual claims, and two of them are double

hands_offscreen is a statement about pixels and nothing else. It cannot be assigned from the narration, which describes the hands whether or not the camera can see them.

audio_only is two claims: the sound identifies the activity and the pixels do not. Checking only the first half ships items that a frame answers trivially, which is exactly the population this flag exists to exclude. Both halves are checked, and the item carries which frames were looked at.

purpose ships only where narration or speech licenses it (§6). Vision can refute a purpose; it can rarely confirm one. So vision is used here as a veto, not as a source.

7.2 The boundary rejection in §3.1 needs frames

The rule rejects a placement when the question end lands within 1 s of a boundary and the two neighbouring spans describe different activities. "Describe different activities" is currently a text comparison on a corpus where 55% of adjacent spans already share less than half their words — the text will say different constantly. Whether the activity actually changed is a question about the frames either side, and it is settled there.

7.3 Control and disagreement

The verifier must be shown finding the activity on frames where the activity is known present before any "not visible" verdict is believed — otherwise a silent detector manufactures hands_offscreen items at will. Where narration and frames disagree, the item is quarantined with the disagreement written down, never deleted and never repaired into agreement.

8. Scoring

The answer is free text, so there are two layers:

  1. Automatic — a canonical (verb, object) pair is extracted from the key and from the model's answer, and scored as a pair. Cheap, runs on every item, catches the gross failures.
  2. Judge — a blind rubric pass on the full sentence, for phrasing and for the cases where the pair is right but the sentence is wrong.

Both are reported. The pair score alone rewards keyword emission; the judge alone is not reproducible across runs.

Report the two populations separately: plain items, and items carrying any of the three hard flags. A single blended number hides whether the model can do anything beyond captioning.

11. Open

  1. The segmenter does not exist. Everything at activity level waits on it.
  2. How many items survive each hard flag is unmeasured, because the flags need the segmenter and a vision pass.
  3. Whether the "anchor span or the one before" rule matches human judgement is untested. A small hand audit should settle it before the family is built on top of it.
